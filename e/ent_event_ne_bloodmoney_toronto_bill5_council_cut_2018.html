Ontario Bill 5 cut Toronto council from 47 to 25 wards mid-campaign, overriding the city's planned 2018 ward structure

Public records ◔Listed in public records

Toronto's 2018 municipal election was under way on a 47-ward map when the province passed the Better Local Government Act, 2018 (Bill 5), cutting council to 25 wards. A Superior Court judge struck the law down on Sept. 10, 2018; the Court of Appeal stayed that ruling and the election went ahead on 25 wards; the Supreme Court of Canada later upheld the law 5-4. Stopped (as reported): Bill 5 received royal assent Aug. 14, 2018 and imposed 25 wards. Belobaba J. struck it down Sept. 10, 2018; the Court of Appeal for Ontario stayed that order Sept. 19, 2018 (2018 ONCA 761), so the vote proceeded on 25 wards; the SCC upheld the Act Oct. 1, 2021 (2021 SCC 34) (by: Government of Ontario (Premier Doug Ford) via Bill 5; Court of Appeal for Ontario stay; Supreme Court of Canada).
