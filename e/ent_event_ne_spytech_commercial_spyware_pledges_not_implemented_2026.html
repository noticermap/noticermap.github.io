Canada's commercial-spyware pledges (2023 Joint Statement, 2024 Pall Mall) not implemented domestically (as of 2026)

Public records ◔Listed in public records

Canada endorsed the March 30, 2023 Joint Statement on Efforts to Counter the Proliferation and Misuse of Commercial Spyware (listed again in the September 22, 2024 update), which commits signatories to guardrails on government spyware use and to preventing exports to likely malicious end-users. Canada is also listed among 'States and international organisations represented' at the Pall Mall Process declaration (Lancaster House, 6 February 2024). Canada is not among the 'Supporting States' of the Pall Mall Process Code of Practice for States (GOV.UK page updated 23 October 2025, retrieved 2026-10-29). On March 23, 2026, Citizen Lab director Ronald Deibert told SDIR that Canada has not put in place export controls or restrictions on mercenary surveillance vendors and has 'done almost nothing' to live up to its pledge. No official federal follow-up (spyware-specific export control, procurement ban or vendor list) was located. Distinct from the existing 2023 ban-list non-event (ETHI Rec 2, government response 2023-03-23). What stopped it: As of March 23, 2026, testimony to Parliament states Canada has no export controls or restrictions on mercenary surveillance vendors and has done almost nothing on the pledge; Canada is not listed as a supporting State of the Pall Mall Code of Practice. No government explanation located.
