New Brunswick ends the stand-alone Right to Information and Privacy Commissioner, folding its role into a new Integrity Commissioner (Act Dec 2016, effective Sept 1, 2017)

Public records ◔Listed in public records

The Integrity Commissioner Act received royal assent on December 16, 2016. It deemed the Conflict of Interest Commissioner, Alexandre Deschênes, the first Integrity Commissioner. From September 1, 2017 it transferred to him the rights and responsibilities of the Right to Information and Protection of Privacy Commissioner, ending that stand-alone office (Integrity Commissioner annual report). Before this, the conflict-of-interest post had been filled on an interim basis for a year by Anne Bertrand, the privacy commissioner, after Alfred Landry retired on July 31, 2015. In 2019 the access and privacy oversight was moved again, to the Ombud (per search; not verified). Stopped (as reported): Integrity Commissioner Act: privacy commissioner functions transferred; stand-alone office ended (by: Government of New Brunswick (Liberal); Legislative Assembly).
