    Sources

      About this map

      This map was compiled with the help of AI research assistants from public sources: news reporting, government registries, court records and public filings. Every entry links to its source.

      • Items marked "reported" have not been independently verified and may contain errors.
      • Labels shown come from the cited source, not from this project.
      • "Leads" show timing patterns in public records and are not evidence of wrongdoing.
      • Nothing here alleges that any person broke the law unless a court or official body has found so.

      Spot an error? Use the "Report a correction" link on any page and we'll review it promptly.

      This is an interactive map of people, organizations, lobby groups, funders, companies and legislation connected through money and influence in Canada. It is built from the reporting of Wiretap Media and FringeViews, shown as separate source layers. Where possible, each claim is then checked against official Canadian public records such as the federal Registry of Lobbyists, provincial lobbyist registries, Elections Canada and provincial election finance records, CRA charity returns, and corporate registries.

      How to read the badges

      Important: claims labelled Reported by … (for example Reported by Wiretap, Reported by FringeViews or Reported by The Bureau) come from that outlet's reporting and have not been independently verified. A connection on this map does not mean wrongdoing. Not found means a records check came up empty. It does not mean the claim is false, because many relationships never show up in a public registry.

      Source layers

      The map combines separate source layers:

        Each colour is chosen to stay distinct for colour-blind readers and each chip also carries a letter. The Source layers toggle shows any combination; all layers are on by default. Every claim and every source in a profile shows its layer chip.

        Seen in N layers. An item is counted in a layer only if that layer reports the item itself. An entity also counts the layers of its own links. A public-record source attached to a Wiretap claim is evidence for that claim; it does not add the Public records layer. Use Seen in at least to show only items found by 2 or more layers (up to the number of layers), and highlight by number of layers to draw a thicker ring on those nodes. The Overlaps view can rank items by cross-layer corroboration. Being seen in several layers means several sources mention an item; it is not proof that a claim is true.

        Duplicates. The map does no name or alias matching of its own. The only authority on which ids are the same entity is the reviewed graph/entity_map.json, which mirrors the project's dedupe tool. Every link id is kept.

        A Reported by … badge means the claim comes from that source's own reporting and has not been independently verified. The other badges show the result of a check against public records. Including a layer is not an endorsement of any outlet's conclusions.

        Leads

        The Leads view lists timing patterns in public data: a donation or funding followed by a contract or grant to a related party; lobbying followed by an award or by a bill moving; a person moving from government to a firm that then lobbied that body. Some are flagged by the records collector, others are detected by the map, and each card says which. Every lead is labelled “Timing pattern”: a timing pattern in public data is not evidence of wrongdoing. Leads never carry a verified badge; each link inside it keeps its own badge and sources. Download leads.csv or leads.json.

        Legal status (people)

        Where a source states a person's legal status, the profile shows a neutral grey chip: Accused Charged Convicted Acquitted or Status unknown, copied exactly as the source states it and never upgraded, with the detail, source link and date beside it. For accused or charged people the profile adds: Allegations have not been proven in court. An acquittal is shown just as prominently. No chip means no stated status; it says nothing either way. Legal status is never a graph colour or a ranking factor; the only filter is has stated legal status.

        Money, estimates, budgets and other amounts

        Amounts are in Canadian dollars. Each amount has a kind: payment, donation, contract, grant, estimate, budget or other. Estimate, Budget and Other amount are not counted as money that changed hands. They are tagged and left out of money totals and of Follow the money unless you tick include estimates, budgets & other. Links with no amount are never added up.

        Guide: tours, explanations and next steps

        The Tours tab plays short guided walks through the map (use ← → or the buttons; Esc exits). How are these connected? finds up to three shortest chains of sourced links between two entities. A claim's Explain this link panel uses the Map Guide's plain-language explanation where there is one, or an automatically generated sentence that is labelled as such. Values shown there are always the live map values. Press Help to replay the first-visit walkthrough.

        Networks and clusters

        Source labels. Some sources attach their own label to an item. Profiles quote it as “Label used by <source>”, with a link, as a third-party claim, never as a category, colour, filter, facet or network. Labels naming a religion, ethnicity or community identity are never shown on people, and on organizations only when the organization’s own name or summary uses the same term.

        Networks are curated groupings, such as board interlocks, shared funders, coalitions, donor circles and revolving doors. Each one has its own sources and status badge. Auto-detected clusters are worked out from link structure alone and are always labelled as auto-detected. They are not findings.

        Overlaps (cross-check)

        The Overlaps view and each profile's Overlaps section list connections found by the map, not reported claims: shared funders (with totals), shared recipients, shared people (board interlocks, employment, revolving doors), the same province, membership of the same network, being named in the same source, and money paths of 2–3 hops. Each overlap links to the sourced claims behind it, and those claims keep their own badges. Overlaps have no status badge and never count toward money totals. Strength scores give less weight to hubs that connect to almost everything (for example the Government of Canada). The path finder shows the shortest chain of sourced links between any two entities. The full cross-check report, including likely duplicate entities and large reported payments still to verify, is at data/crosscheck.json, and overlaps can be downloaded as overlaps.csv.

        Methodology

        1. Extraction. Entities (people in their public roles, organizations, bills…) and the links between them are pulled from Wiretap articles. Every entity and link cites its source article.
        2. Records check. A checker searches the relevant public registries and attaches the record (registry name, record ID, link). It then sets a status: verified, partly verified, not found or contradicted.
        3. Dates. Links can be dated to a year, a month or a day. The date filter keeps a link if its period overlaps the range. You can filter by the event date or by when Wiretap first reported it.
        4. Scope. The map covers public figures and organizations in their public roles only. It includes no home addresses, family details, ethnicity or religion.
